\chapter{General Conclusions}\label{conclusions}
\chapterquote{GROMACS reminds you: ``A protein is a chain of letters.''}{Julie Bernauer}
\newpage
This doctoral thesis presents a detailed computational study of CP self-assembly and the interactions of the resulting supramolecular species (SCPNs) with lipid bilayers of varying compositions. Using molecular simulation techniques, Big Data analysis and Machine Learning models, the activity of CPs in biologically relevant scenarios was assessed, trying to elucidate some of the factors that dictate their behavior and providing the scientific community with tools to simplify and accelerate CP research.

The first major set of results, presented in Section \ref{chap1}, focused on overcoming fundamental limitations of existing coarse-grained (CG) force fields for modeling CP self-assembly. Through the development of MA(R/S)TINI and its subsequent refinement MA(R/S)TINI 3, this thesis demonstrated that introducing two additional backbone beads to mimic \textit{N-H} and \textit{C=O} groups successfully restores hydrogen-bond directionality, a key feature governing CP stacking. These modifications addressed critical artifacts of the original Martini force field, including excessive inter-ring distances, the inability to distinguish between parallel and antiparallel \(\beta\)-sheet arrangements, and the formation of non-physical rotational orientations that would lead to donor–acceptor mismatches at atomistic resolution. In particular, MA(R/S)TINI 3 leveraged the expanded bead set of Martini 3 to achieve dimerization free-energy profiles closely aligned with those of the original force field, while preserving the geometric advantages introduced by the chirality-aware mapping. This refinement also enabled the removal of artificial restraints on side chains, allowing for more realistic conformational flexibility and improved structural fidelity of self-assembled nanotubes. Both adapted force fields, along with the original versions and AA force fields, were integrated into CYCLOPEp platform, a web tool that allows the construction of CPs and SCPNs, providing 3D structures for visualization and ready-to-use files for MD.

Building on these methodological advances, Section \ref{chap2}, exploited MA(R/S)TINI 3 and the associated structure-generation tools to perform thousands of CG-MD simulations of SCPNs in the presence of a negatively charged bacterial membrane model. From these simulations, more than 1,000 structural and energetic descriptors were extracted and stored in a database. Unsupervised analysis of this database revealed three distinct classes of CP-membrane interactions---Lipophilic, Intermediate and Lipophobic---each characterized by specific patterns of membrane association, contact formation, and interaction energetics. Sequence-level analysis showed that these interaction modes correlated strongly with amino acid composition and charge distribution rather than net charge alone. Lipophilic peptides were enriched in basic residues while maintaining near-neutral overall charge, favoring stable membrane association. Lipophobic peptides were dominated by acidic residues and strongly negative charges, leading to electrostatic exclusion from the bilayer. Intermediate peptides exhibited more heterogeneous behavior, combining hydrophobic and charged residues in a way that promoted transient or partial membrane interactions. To evaluate the predictive utility of the MD simulation-derived dataset, a MPNN was trained on graphs representations encoding both force field parameters and topological features. The resulting model showed strong performance in both classification and regression tasks, demonstrating that physically grounded MD data can serve as an effective foundation for ML-driven prediction. All simulation data and analyses were made publicly available through CYCLOPEpDB, enabling reuse and further exploration by the community.

The publication of CycPeptMPDB, a database containing experimental permeability data of over 7,000 cyclic peptide sequences formed by a wide variety of natural and synthetic residues, opened 
the door to train ML models to make predictions on experimental data. The focus of the work presented in Section \ref{chap3}, was to investigate how chemical intuition impacts predictive accuracy, trying to optimize it by means of different representation schemes, data augmentation strategies and NN architectures. The results showed that representations based on amino acid order and global peptide properties outperformed SMILES-based encodings in this context, underscoring the importance of chemically informed representations. Exploiting the circular nature of CPs through cyclic permutations yielded modest improvements, whereas amino acid substitution-based augmentation---even when chemically conservative---consistently degraded performance. These findings highlight the sensitivity of cyclic peptide activity to sequence composition and emphasize that data consistency and curation are more critical than dataset size alone. Architectural modifications designed to explicitly encode cyclicity offered limited advantages over standard convolutional approaches, reinforcing the conclusion that representation quality outweighs architectural complexity. The best-performing model achieved predictive accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art methods reported in the literature and was deployed as the web-based tool CYCLOPS within the CYCLOPEp platform, enabling non-expert users to rapidly estimate CP membrane permeability.

Summing up, all the work developed in this thesis has been gathered in CYCLOPEp, an open source integrated online platform comprising three complementary components: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CYCLOPEpBuilder}: Construction and visualization of 3D structures of \textit{D,L-}$\alpha$-CPs and SCPNs, either for MD simulation or structure visualization.
    \item \textbf{CYCLOPEpDB}: An extensive, curated database of CG-MD simulations describing \textit{D,L-}$\alpha$-CPs interactions with a bacterial membrane model, enabling both direct analysis and ML applications.
    \item \textbf{CYCLOPS}: Evaluation of CP permeability for a wide variety of amino acid residues and sequence lengths.
\end{itemize}
Overall, this work demonstrates how carefully adapted molecular models, combined with automated simulation pipelines and data-driven analysis, can bridge the gap between mechanistic insight and large-scale prediction. By integrating Molecular Dynamics and Machine Learning within a unified and accessible framework, this thesis provides a solid foundation for the rational exploration and design of biologically active cyclic peptides. We anticipate that CYCLOPEp will serve as a valuable resource for the scientific community, facilitating access to advanced computational methodologies and contributing to the development of more effective cyclic peptide-based antimicrobial strategies.
